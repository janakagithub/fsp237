# Storage carbohydrate metabolism (glycogen / trehalose)

## 1. Finding

Across the 18-condition media panel, storage-carbohydrate reactions carry flux in a
majority of conditions at every stage, with a **modest, pre-infection-leaning
stage-differential signal**:

| Metric | Pre-infection | Biotrophic | Necrotrophic |
|---|---|---|---|
| Active-rate (fraction of conditions with non-zero flux) | **0.3143** | 0.2857 | 0.2857 |

- **Stage-differential (flux):** active-rate range across stages = **0.0286** — small in
  absolute terms, but the ordering is consistent: storage turnover is engaged slightly
  *more often* pre-infection than during biotrophy or necrotrophy.
- **Method-discordant:** `mean_method_range` = **0.2857** — moderately high disagreement
  among the transcriptomics-integration methods (pFBA vs E-Flux / GIMME / iMAT) about
  whether, and how much, these reactions carry flux.

Because a single static transcriptome (S1) is applied to every medium, the pre-infection
enrichment here is **medium/flux-driven, not expression-driven** (see Caveats).

## 2. Mechanistic interpretation

Glycogen and trehalose are the two dominant carbon reserves in filamentous
phytopathogenic fungi, and both are classically **mobilized during spore germination and
appressorium development** — precisely the events that a "pre-infection" medium regime is
meant to approximate. Three well-established biological threads make a slightly elevated
*pre-infection* storage-turnover signal the biologically expected result:

1. **Reserve mobilization fuels germination and appressorium morphogenesis.** The dormant
   conidium arrives carbon-starved on the host surface; germination and the building of a
   penetration structure are bankrolled by breakdown of pre-stored glycogen and lipid
   rather than by exogenous nutrients. In the closely related grass pathogen
   *Colletotrichum graminicola* (maize anthracnose, a sister disease to sorghum
   anthracnose), lipid bodies are actively mobilized as the conidium exits dormancy and
   forms an appressorium — direct evidence that reserve turnover peaks at this
   pre-penetration stage [Tier 1]. In *Magnaporthe oryzae*, glycogen and triacylglycerol
   deposits are translocated from the conidium into the incipient appressorium and rapidly
   degraded during turgor generation [Tier 2].

2. **Storage catabolism is the osmolyte/turgor engine for penetration.** Appressorial
   turgor is generated by accumulating a compatible osmolyte (chiefly glycerol) behind a
   melanized, semi-permeable wall; the carbon skeletons for that glycerol come from
   degradation of stored glycogen and lipid. MAP-kinase (Pmk1) and PKA signalling drive
   the coordinated mobilization of glycogen and triacylglycerol during turgor generation
   in *M. oryzae* [Tier 2]. This one-time, pre-penetration burst of storage catabolism is
   a natural explanation for storage reactions reading *more active pre-infection* than in
   the intracellular biotrophic/necrotrophic phases, where the fungus instead draws on
   host-derived nutrients.

3. **Trehalose spans synthesis and degradation at different infection stages, and doubles
   as a stress protectant / virulence factor.** In *M. oryzae*, trehalose *synthesis*
   (Tps1) is required early (conidiogenesis, germination, appressorium function) while
   trehalose *degradation* (the neutral trehalase Nth1) matters at later infection stages
   — genetic evidence that the pathway's net flux direction is itself stage-dependent
   [Tier 2]. Tps1 additionally acts as a metabolic/redox sensor (linking glucose-6-P and
   NADPH status to pathogenicity) and trehalose accumulation protects against oxidative
   and desiccation stress encountered on the leaf surface and during the host oxidative
   burst [Tier 2]. Elevated pre-infection storage turnover is therefore consistent with
   both the turgor role and the stress-protectant role concentrated at the surface/
   germination stage.

**Why methods disagree (the 0.2857 method range).** Storage metabolism is built from
**reversible / cyclic synthesis-degradation pairs** (glycogen synthase ↔ glycogen
phosphorylase; trehalose-6-P synthase/phosphatase ↔ trehalase). A near-futile
synthesis/degradation cycle can be satisfied by many equivalent flux solutions, so
methods that reward carrying expression-supported flux (E-Flux) versus methods that prune
low-expression reactions or binarize activity (GIMME, iMAT) can land on very different
active/inactive calls for the same reaction set. GPR ambiguity compounds this: trehalase
and glycogen-branching/debranching steps often have isoenzyme or loosely annotated GPRs,
so a single expression prior maps inconsistently onto the reactions. The result is
exactly the pattern seen — a modest, robust *directional* stage signal riding on top of
*method-level* disagreement about magnitude and on/off status.

## 4. Caveats

- **Single-transcriptome limitation (decisive here).** Only the S1 pathogenic-state
  transcriptome exists, applied statically to every medium. There is **no germination /
  appressorium-specific expression state** in these data, so the model cannot reproduce
  the *transcriptional* up-regulation of trehalose/glycogen genes that the literature ties
  to the pre-infection stage. The pre-infection lean (0.3143 vs 0.2857) is therefore a
  **medium-driven artifact of substrate availability**, not evidence of stage-specific
  regulation — it is biologically *consistent* with reserve mobilization at germination,
  but it does not independently demonstrate it. No differential expression, no p-values.
- **Small effect size.** An active-rate range of 0.0286 corresponds to roughly one
  condition out of the panel flipping on/off; it should be reported as suggestive, not
  robust.
- **Reversible-cycle / GPR artifacts drive the method discordance.** The high
  `mean_method_range` (0.2857) most likely reflects alternate-optima freedom in reversible
  synthesis/degradation loops and ambiguous trehalase/glycogen-enzyme GPRs, rather than a
  real biological switch. Loopless-FBA or explicit thermodynamic/directionality
  constraints would sharpen the call.
- **Turgor/osmolyte physiology is not captured by FBA.** The core biological payoff of
  storage catabolism — osmotic turgor generation — is a biophysical outcome the stoichio-
  metric model does not represent; flux through the pathway is at best a proxy.
- **Alternative reading.** The near-constant biotrophic vs necrotrophic active-rate
  (both 0.2857) is equally consistent with storage metabolism being a housekeeping
  carbon buffer that FBA keeps partially active regardless of stage, with the medium
  merely nudging the pre-infection value upward.

## 5. Confidence

**Medium.** The underlying biology (glycogen/trehalose mobilization for germination and
appressorial turgor; stage-split trehalose synthesis vs degradation) is very well
established and directly supported in *Colletotrichum* and *Magnaporthe* (Tiers 1–2). But
the specific quantitative signal is small (range 0.0286), method-discordant (0.2857), and
cannot be expression-corroborated given the single static transcriptome — so the *model*
result is only weakly diagnostic even though its *direction* matches expectation.
